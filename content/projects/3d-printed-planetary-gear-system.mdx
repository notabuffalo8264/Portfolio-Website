### How This Started

This was my **ME 470 Honors Option Project** for Mechanical Design and Manufacturing II at Michigan State University.

I wanted to make something that was more than just a written analysis, so I designed and built a small **planetary gear system** that could physically demonstrate gear reduction and torque amplification. The final project combined the theory side of gear trains with CAD, simulation, and a 3D-printed working model.

## What I Made

The mechanism includes a sun gear, planet gears, ring gear, carrier, base, and knob. I designed the parts in **SOLIDWORKS**, checked the gear relationships with a small **Python model**, and then used a SolidWorks motion study to verify that the CAD behaved the way I expected.

After that, I printed the parts in **PLA-CF** and assembled the physical gear system. The final model was self-contained and could be spun by hand to show the motion of the planetary gear train.

## My Contributions

Since this was an individual honors option project, I completed the full process myself:

- Designed the planetary gear assembly and individual parts in SOLIDWORKS.
- Created a Python model to compare gear configurations before finalizing the design.
- Ran a SolidWorks motion study to verify the expected gear motion.
- 3D printed the mechanism using PLA-CF filament.
- Assembled and tested the physical gear system by hand.
- Wrote the final report documenting the design, analysis, CAD, manufacturing, and testing process.

## Building the Physical Model

The main challenge was making the gears work well after printing. Since 3D printing introduces small tolerance errors, the design needed enough clearance to avoid binding while still keeping the teeth engaged cleanly.

Once everything was printed, I laid out the individual parts, assembled the carrier and planet gears, and tested the mechanism by rotating it by hand. The finished model showed the expected gear motion and gave me a much better physical understanding of how planetary gear trains behave.

## Motion Validation

I also created a CAD motion study so I could compare the digital model against the physical one. The CAD version helped confirm that the gear mates and motion relationships were working before relying on the printed parts.

This made the project feel more complete because it was not just a printed model — it connected the analysis, simulation, and real hardware into one working demonstration.
