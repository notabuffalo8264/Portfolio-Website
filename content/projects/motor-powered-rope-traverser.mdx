### How This Started

This was the final project for **ME 470: Mechanical Design and Manufacturing II** at Michigan State University. Our team of four was challenged to design and build a robot capable of autonomously traversing a suspended 10-meter rope while fitting inside a standard shoebox before deployment.

The project took us through the complete engineering process—from brainstorming and CAD to machining, manufacturing, testing, and plenty of redesigns along the way.

## The Design

Our robot used two synchronized **Grashof four-bar linkages** that alternated gripping and pulling along the rope. A DC motor powered both linkage systems through a gear reduction, converting continuous rotation into a walking motion.

Over the semester we continuously refined nearly every part of the design, adjusting the linkage geometry, improving packaging, redesigning components, and manufacturing multiple iterations until we had a robot that was ready for Design Day.

## My Contributions

I spent most of my time working on the mechanical design of the robot.

Some of my main contributions included:

- Creating and updating much of the CAD as the design evolved throughout the semester.
- Using my personal 3D printer to manufacture many of the custom plastic parts, allowing us to quickly prototype new revisions.
- Performing the complete **finite element analysis (FEA)** of the drive shaft in Abaqus and comparing the results against analytical calculations.
- Helping machine, assemble, and test the final robot leading up to Design Day.

## Biggest Challenge

One of the biggest goals throughout the project was making the four-bar linkage move as smoothly as possible. We spent most of the semester refining the geometry until the motion looked great—but there was one problem: the robot had become far too slow.

With only a few weeks remaining before Design Day, we decided to redesign a large portion of the project. That meant updating the CAD, machining new parts, reprinting nearly every custom component, and rebuilding the robot under a very tight deadline.

It made for a stressful final stretch, but it was also one of the most rewarding parts of the project. Watching the redesigned robot finally come together after so many late nights made all of the extra work worth it.
